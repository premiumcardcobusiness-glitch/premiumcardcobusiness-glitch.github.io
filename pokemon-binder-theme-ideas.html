Binder theme ideas

12 Pokémon binder themes to try before you sleeve a card.

A themed page is the one people stop on when you open the binder. Here are twelve themes collectors build, and a free page builder to try yours as nine pockets, as many times as you like, before you move a single card.

Try a theme free

The classic: one Pokémon, nine printings

Pick a favorite and fill a page with nine different printings of it, old and new, or give it a whole binder. For Pikachu in date order, try the Pikachu timeline page; the Charizard page does the same for Charizard. These five pages were each built in under a minute on the free page builder. Swipe them, or tap one to start your own.

Eleven more themes

  1. Eevee and its evolutions. Eevee plus its eight evolutions fill exactly nine pockets: Vaporeon, Jolteon, Flareon, Espeon, Umbreon, Leafeon, Glaceon and Sylveon around Eevee in the middle. Eevee family page guide
  2. Starter lines. A page is three rows of three, so the three starters of one region fit one evolution line per row: Bulbasaur, Charmander and Squirtle down the left, their final forms down the right. Starter page guide. Legendary trios fill a page the same way, one trio per row. Legendary page guide
  3. One type. A fire page, a water page, a ghost page. Cards of one type share a frame color, so the page reads as one block from across the room.
  4. A color fade. Order cards by color, red to orange to yellow and on, so the whole page shifts as your eye moves across it.
  5. Full arts only. Full-art and illustration rare cards run the artwork to the edge. A page of nine looks like a gallery wall.
  6. One artist. The illustrator's name is printed at the bottom of every card. Pick one whose style you love and collect a page of their work.
  7. One region. Only Pokémon first found in one region, like Kanto's original 151, in Pokédex order.
  8. A place or a story. A farm page, an ocean page, a night-time page: Pokémon that belong in the same scene, placed so the page tells it.
  9. Shiny Pokémon. Shiny cards show the rare color version of a Pokémon. A page of them stands out from every regular page around it.
  10. A finished set. One set, in card-number order, page after page. The last empty pocket is the one you will remember filling.
  11. Your childhood page. The cards you had as a kid, or the ones you always wanted. If you found your old binder, start with its best nine.

Try a theme before you move a card

  1. Open the free page builder. Pick Pikachu, Eevee, Gengar, Mewtwo or Charizard, or type any Pokémon's name.
  2. Tap the cards you want, in the order you want them in the pockets. Type another name to mix Pokémon on one page, the way the Eevee and starter themes need.
  3. Tap See page. Tap a card to take it off, or Add cards to try another. Change it until it feels right.
  4. Send page gives you a link with a picture of the page. Keep page saves it as a binder in a free account.

The builder covers One Piece and Dragon Ball cards too, see the binder page planner.

Questions

How many cards fit on a binder page?

A standard page has nine pockets in three rows of three. Some binders use four-pocket or twelve-pocket pages; the page builder plans the nine-pocket kind.

Is the page builder free?

Yes. Planning a page needs no account. Keeping it makes a free account, which never asks for payment details.

Does it work on my phone?

Yes, on iPhone and Android in the browser, and on a computer. There is nothing to download.
