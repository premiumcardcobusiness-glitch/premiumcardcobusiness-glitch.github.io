Privacy Policy

Last updated: 12 October 2026

This Privacy Policy explains what information Beltstra Co ("we", "us"), a sole proprietorship based in Aubrey, Texas, USA, collects and how we use it.

1. Information we collect

Payment information

We do not collect or store your card or bank details. All payments are processed by our Merchant of Record, Paddle.com, who collects the information needed to complete your purchase (such as name, email, billing country, and payment details) under their own privacy policy.

License information

To operate your membership we store a license identifier associated with your purchase and the email address you used, so we can issue and verify your license. When you sign in or scan, we receive your license token to confirm it is active.

Card images and usage

When you scan a card, the image is sent to our servers to identify the card and is deleted after processing — we do not keep your card photos. We record per-scan usage (a count, timestamp, and basic device/connection information) to enforce your plan's scan allowance and to detect account sharing or automated abuse.

Links page visits

When you open our links page (the page our printed QR code opens) or tap one of its buttons, your browser sends a small visit notice to our server. We record the time, which button was tapped, your browser type, and your network address with its last part removed. We use this only to count visits. The links page itself sets no cookies and stores nothing on your device. The app pages its buttons lead to may keep things there, such as the cards you price or an ID (see "App visits and error reports" below).

Short link visits

Some shared binders have a short link on beltstra.com, such as beltstra.com/premiumcardco. It sends you on to the binder in the app. When the binder opens, the page tells our server, as it does for every shared binder link, and the server counts the visit for the binder's owner, noting that it came through the short link and whether it opened inside Instagram's app. To count each visitor once, it keeps a coded form of your network address and browser type, never the address itself, for about four months. The short link page itself sets no cookies and stores nothing on your device.

App visits and error reports

We use PostHog to count visits, sign-ups and orders, and Sentry to find and fix errors. Here is what PostHog gets from your browser:

  • Which pages. PostHog runs only on these pages of app.beltstra.com: "What's your page worth?" (a binder page priced from one photo) and its version for card shops, "Binder offer in 60 seconds"; "Had Pokémon cards as a kid?" and the results people share from it; and the sign-in and sign-up page. It does not run anywhere else: not on our website beltstra.com or its links page, not on the one-card price check ("What's this card worth?"), and not in the rest of the app.
  • What it notes. Only two moments: when one of those pages opens, and when you start to sign up. Each note has the page address, the site or link that sent you, and basic details about your browser and device, such as its type, screen size, language and time zone. It does not note your other taps or what you type, and it does not record your screen.
  • What is taken out. Before a note leaves your phone, the page takes out email addresses, the codes in shared links, and everything after a "?" or "#" in an address, except campaign tags that an ad or a post adds to a link, such as utm_source. Those tags are still sent.
  • What is kept. A random ID, which tells a return visit from a new one, and the full address of the page where your visit started and of the page or site that sent you there. They are kept in a cookie and in your browser's storage.
  • Who gets it. Your browser sends the notes straight to PostHog, so PostHog also gets your network address and may use it to estimate your rough location. The cookie also goes to our own sites when you visit them, and our server reads the random ID from it when you sign up, start a checkout or use a free price check.
  • Instagram and Facebook. Inside Instagram's or Facebook's own browser, the page adds the random ID to the end of its address, after a "#", so that if you open the page in Safari or Chrome we count one visitor, not two. The part after a "#" is never sent to our server or to PostHog. Safari or Chrome takes it off the address as soon as the page opens, and uses it only if it is less than an hour old and that browser has no random ID of its own. Instagram's or Facebook's browser can see the address, including that ID. If you copy the address there and someone opens it within the hour, their visit may be counted with yours.

Our server also tells PostHog when you make an account; when you save your first card, and each time you save a card; when you make your first binder, and each time you make a binder; each time you scan cards or post a show table; and when you start a checkout or pay for an order. If you scan with a free price check before you have an account, our server notes that scan too, under the random ID from your visits when your browser has one. These notes carry only plain details, such as how you signed up, which screen a scan came from and how many cards a free scan read, whether a saved card is new to your collection or another copy, how many pockets a binder page has, how many days a show table stays up, or what you bought and its price. They never say which cards you have. We never send PostHog your name or email address: it knows your account by a coded ID. When you sign up, that coded ID is linked to the random ID from your visits, so PostHog can tie your account to those visits and to your rough location.

Sentry gets a report when something breaks. Only our server sends reports to Sentry: if the app hits an error on your phone while you are signed in, the app tells our server, which may pass it on. A report says what went wrong, where in our code, on which page and in which browser. Our server is set up to leave out your account, cookies, network address and the forms and photos you send us, and to remove email addresses, sign-in codes, the codes in shared links and payment IDs before a report is sent.

Your eBay account, if you connect it

If you connect your eBay account, we keep the sign-in token eBay gives us, so the Service can create the listings you ask for and show your open eBay orders on the Pack orders screen. We only read your orders; we never change them. While you pack, that screen shows each buyer's first name and last initial so you can match the order to its envelope. We do not store buyer names or addresses. When you mark an order packed, we keep its order number, the listing title and what eBay pays you for the card, so your collection and profit stay correct. You can remove the Service's access at any time in your eBay account settings.

Friends

You can add friends in the app by scanning each other's friend code in person. The code changes every minute and does not contain your email address. Your friends see the name you chose and, unless you choose to keep your cards private, the cards in your collection: card names, printings, how many you have and their market prices. Friends never see what you paid, your cash, cards you sell for other people, or your email address. You can remove a friend at any time.

If you give a card to a friend in the app, that friend sees the card, its market price when you gave it and your name, and the card only moves into their collection if they allow it. They never see what the card cost you. Both of you can see the gift in your own history.

Binders and card pictures

A binder in the app is your own page-by-page arrangement of cards you already have in your collection. While you share your cards, a friend can flip through your binder pages read-only, in the order you arranged them; they never see what you paid. Card pictures are not stored by us: your browser asks the card's own price source (TCGplayer or pokemontcg.io) for the picture, so that site sees the request coming from your browser. The address of the page you are on is not sent with it, and neither is your name or email.

2. How we use information

  • To issue, verify, and support your membership license
  • To respond to support requests you send us
  • To count visits, sign-ups and orders, and to find and fix errors
  • To comply with legal and tax obligations (largely handled by Paddle)

3. Sharing

We share information only with service providers necessary to run the Service — primarily Paddle.com for payment processing. Our service providers also include PostHog, for counting visits, sign-ups and orders, and Sentry, for error reports (see "App visits and error reports" above). We do not sell your personal information.

4. Data retention

We keep license and contact information for as long as your account is active and as needed to meet legal obligations, then delete or anonymize it.

5. Your rights

You may request access to, correction of, or deletion of the personal information we hold about you by emailing us. For payment data held by Paddle, contact Paddle directly.
