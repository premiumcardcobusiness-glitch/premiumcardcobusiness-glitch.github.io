Found an old Pokémon binder? Here's how to see what it's worth.

That binder is a piece of your childhood. Photograph it one page at a time and get it back on your phone: every card named, its market price beside it, and the whole binder ready to show off with a link.

Look up a card free Try a page now, no account

1. Look up one card, no sign-up

Every card has a number at the bottom, like 4/102. Type the card's name or that number on the Card prices page and you'll see each set it was printed in, with today's near-mint market price. Base Set and Base Set (Shadowless) are listed apart, because they sell for very different amounts.

2. Price a whole page in one photo

  1. Lay the binder flat in even light and take one photo of the full nine-pocket page.
  2. Each pocket comes back with the card's name, set and market price, and the page shows its total.
  3. A card the app is not sure of is marked, so you can tap the right printing before you trust the price.
  4. Turn the page and repeat. A 360-card binder is about 40 photos.

3. Check these yourself

  • 1st Edition: a small black "Edition 1" stamp on the left, just below the picture. Card prices here don't list 1st Edition copies apart, so a stamped card is usually worth more than the price shown. Check recent sold listings for that exact card.
  • Shadowless: on the earliest Base Set cards, the frame around the picture has no shadow along its right side.
  • Holo: a shiny picture usually means the rare version of that card.
  • Condition: bends, creases, scratches and white edges lower the price.
  • Graded cards in sealed plastic cases sell by their grade, so price those by grade, not from this list.

Where the prices come from

TCGplayer market prices, refreshed every morning: what copies have sold for lately, not what sellers are asking. They are for clean, ungraded cards, so a worn card sells for less.

Then make it yours again

  • Show it off: share the binder with a link. Friends see the cards and market prices, never what you paid.
  • Plan its next pages: try a theme on the free binder page planner as many times as you like before you sleeve a single card.
  • Keep it current: it is re-priced every morning, and you can download it as a spreadsheet any time.
